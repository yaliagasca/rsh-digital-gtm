B.3 External Market Assessment (§3.2)

B.3.1 Market Demand Validation Approach

The market, buyer, and demand research work breaks down into four components: 1) secondary market and competitive intelligence, 2) primary qualitative research with experts and buyers, 3) primary quantitative validation across the three service pillars, and 4) synthesis into a decision-ready demand evidence base that feeds market sizing, portfolio prioritization, and financial modeling.

1. Secondary Market and Competitive Intelligence

Slalom will review public sources, government and procurement data, industry reports, and technology and infrastructure spending trends to establish the external baseline for Transportation, Aviation, and Federal Defense. Slalom will apply its proprietary AI-enabled growth-opportunity accelerator to scan macro trends, infrastructure investment signals, and the competitive and adjacent landscape at a breadth and scale not achievable through manual research alone, with findings further validated through stakeholder interviews and primary research. The competitive analysis will distinguish direct competitors, substitutes, partners, and internal client alternatives, covering traditional AEC firms, digital-native consultancies, systems integrators, cloud and technology platforms, digital twin providers, asset-management and infrastructure software companies, and OT/cybersecurity specialists. This broader frame is what informs route-to-market and partnership strategy downstream, not just a competitor list.

This same analysis carries a second purpose. RS&H's build/buy/partner mandate means whitespace isn't only a question of where demand exists and no one is serving it. It's also a question of who already occupies that space, and whether the fastest credible path is through organic growth, a partnership, or strategic options (tuck-in acquisition, minority equity stake, joint venture). Slalom will profile adjacent firms that look like plausible strategic or partnership targets based on public signals, ownership structure, size, and any public indication of seeking capital or a strategic partner. So white-space findings arrive with a build-or-acquire lens in addition to analysis on the market gap.

Whitespace hypotheses at this stage will be further screened against the brand and value-proposition criteria established in Weeks 1 to 2 to ensure brand alignment.

Example Outputs: Competitive analysis (direct, substitute, partner, and internal-alternative categories), preliminary whitespace hypotheses, technology and infrastructure investment trend brief.

2. Primary qualitative research: buyer, buying center, and expert interviews

Slalom will conduct up to 15 in-depth interviews across two programs. The buyer and prospective-customer program covers CIO, COO, Chief Digital Officer, Program Director, asset management, data and analytics, innovation, and procurement or contracting roles, distributed across Transportation, Aviation, and Federal Defense. Primary research will focus on current/prospective buyers and independent market experts; Slalom will not conduct competitor or partner interviews.

Buyer interview analysis will support the mapping of the buying committee to provide insights into: who owns budget, who influences the decision, who has to sign off, and where RS&H's existing account relationship sits relative to that structure. Interviews will also help us test problem urgency, budget and timing, standalone-versus-bundled preference, directional price sensitivity, RS&H's right to win, and how buyers respond to AI-enabled delivery within each pillar.

Example Outputs: Buyer in-depth interview discussion guides, recruitment screener, anonymized notes and completed interviews synthesis, buyer profiles, RS&H differentiation and right-to-win evidence by market.

3. Primary quantitative research: pillar-based demand validation survey

To capture directional, quantifiable evidence on packaging preference, bundling appetite, and price sensitivity across RS&H's three service pillars, Slalom will conduct a quantitative survey targeting n=150 to 200 qualified respondents across Transportation, Aviation, and Federal Defense. The survey is designed around RS&H's three service pillars, Digital Advisory & Consulting, Digital Managed Services, and Digital Products & Platforms, rather than split by market. To isolate genuine preference from ordering effects, respondents will assess all three service pillars in randomized order, applying the same three measures to each: standalone-versus-bundled preference, packaging appeal, and directional price sensitivity. Within each pillar module, the survey will also test whether AI-enabled delivery increases packaging appeal or price tolerance.

Target completion is 150 to 200 qualified respondents. The minimum viable threshold will be confirmed with the recruiting partner once incidence and feasibility has been reviewed. Analysis will be presented as aggregate findings with directional comparisons by market.

Example Outputs: Survey instrument and design, survey screener, completed dataset (target n of 150 to 200), pillar-level packaging and pricing findings, quantified problem urgency and purchase-likelihood findings.

4. Market sizing: TAM, SAM, and SOM

Once interviews and survey findings are synthesized, Slalom will size the market using both a top-down (TAM & SAM) and a bottom-up (SOM) view, then reconcile the two into a single defensible range rather than presenting either alone. The top-down view starts from industry spend, infrastructure investment trends, and public-sector budget data across Transportation, Aviation, and Federal Defense, the same secondary sources gathered in component one. The bottom-up view builds from what the primary research found: buyer incidence and purchase likelihood from the survey, average contract value and renewal potential from the interviews, and RS&H's existing account access and realistic win rate.

TAM reflects total demand for the digital problem and offering category across all three markets. SAM narrows that to the accounts, procurement environments, and delivery models RS&H can realistically serve. SOM reflects what RS&H can credibly capture given current account access, delivery capacity, and sales reach. This sits after primary research concludes, so the ranges are grounded in what buyers said rather than desk research alone, and land in time to inform portfolio prioritization and the financial model that follows.

Alongside sizing, Slalom will benchmark the economic patterns typical of the three service pillars, standard customer acquisition cost margin ranges for platform and subscription businesses relevant to Digital Products & Platforms, standard utilization and margin ranges for advisory and managed services models relevant to the other two, drawn from public company filings, investor decks, and published industry studies. This is category-level benchmarking, not a claim about any named competitor’s specific cost structure, since that level of detail is rarely disclosed publicly. It gives the financial model an external reference point particularly for Digital Products & Platforms where the relevant economics look nothing like RS&H's existing AEC cost structure.

Example Outputs: TAM, SAM, and SOM ranges by market and pillar, with documented assumptions, and industry benchmark economics by pillar, and competitive landscape, buyer personas by market, and demand validation for priority offerings across the three identified growth markets.

Expected Outcome: RS&H leadership will receive a transparent, triangulated view of who buys, which problems are urgent enough to fund, how demand and buying-center dynamics differ across Transportation, Aviation, and Federal Defense, which packaging, delivery, and pricing constructs have the strongest evidence of buyer acceptance across the three service pillars, and which white-space opportunities are best pursued organically, through a partner, or through acquisition. Findings are filtered through the brand and value-proposition criteria established at the outset, so the evidence base shows not just where RS&H could win, but where RS&H should.
