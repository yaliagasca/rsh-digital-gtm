Desk research will also produce preliminary whitespace hypotheses: underserved problems, emerging demand, and areas of limited competitive differentiation. These are hypotheses at this stage, not conclusions. They get tested and either confirmed or revised through the expert and buyer interviews in the next component. Secondary intelligence runs from Week 1 through Week 5, so early findings inform offering concept development before primary research concludes.

Key deliverables: Competitive analysis (direct, substitute, partner, and internal-alternative categories), preliminary whitespace hypotheses, technology and infrastructure investment trend brief.

2. Primary qualitative research: buyer, buying-center, and expert interviews

Slalom will conduct 20 to 25 in-depth interviews across two programs. The buyer and prospective-customer program covers CIO, COO, Chief Digital Officer, Program Director, asset-management, data and analytics, innovation, and procurement or contracting roles, distributed across Transportation, Aviation, and Federal Defense. The industry expert program draws from digital and technology leaders across transportation, aviation, and federal acquisition, digital twin and smart-infrastructure specialists, and former procurement leaders, supplementing direct buyer access where it is limited.

Buyer interview analysis will support the mapping of the buying committee to provide insights into: who owns budget, who influences the decision, who has to sign off, and where RS&H's existing account relationship sits relative to that structure. Interviews will also help us test problem urgency, budget and timing, standalone-versus-bundled preference, directional price sensitivity and RS&H's right to win.

Research plan finalization, discussion guides, and screener development happen in Week 1. Recruitment begins Week 2, supplemented by Slalom's third-party research recruitment and incentive partner network to extend reach beyond RS&H's existing relationships. Fielding runs Weeks 3 through 6, including a short pilot round of two to three interviews to validate the guide and screener before full fielding.

Key deliverables: Buyer & industry expert in-depth interview discussion guides, recruitment screener, anonymized notes and synthesis of 20 to 25 completed interviews synthesis, buyer profiles, RS&H differentiation and right-to-win evidence by market.

3. Primary quantitative research: pillar-based demand validation survey

To capture directional, quantifiable evidence on packaging preference, bundling appetite, and price sensitivity across RS&H's three service pillars, Slalom will conduct a quantitative survey targeting n=150 to 200 qualified respondents across Transportation, Aviation, and Federal Defense. The survey is designed around RS&H's three service pillars, Digital Advisory & Consulting, Digital Managed Services, and Digital Products & Platforms, rather than split by market. To isolate genuine preference from ordering effects, respondents will assess all three service pillars in randomized order, applying the same three measures to each: standalone-versus-bundled preference, packaging appeal, and directional price sensitivity.

Target completion is 150 to 200 qualified respondents. The minimum viable threshold will be confirmed with the recruiting partner once incidence and feasibility has been reviewed. Analysis will be presented as aggregate findings with directional comparisons by market.

Example Outputs: Survey instrument and design, survey screener, completed dataset (target n of 150 to 200), pillar-level packaging and pricing findings, quantified problem urgency and purchase-likelihood findings.

4. Market sizing: TAM, SAM, and SOM

Once interviews and survey data are in, Slalom will size the market using both a top-down and a bottom-up view, then reconcile the two into a single defensible range rather than presenting either alone. The top-down view starts from industry spend, infrastructure investment trends, and public-sector budget data across Transportation, Aviation, and Federal Defense, the same secondary sources gathered in component one. The bottom-up view builds from what the primary research actually found: buyer incidence and purchase likelihood from the survey, average contract value and renewal potential from the interviews, and RS&H's existing account access and realistic win rate.

TAM reflects total demand for the digital problem and offering category across all three markets. SAM narrows that to the accounts, procurement environments, and delivery models RS&H can realistically serve. SOM reflects what RS&H can credibly capture given current account access, delivery capacity, and sales reach. This sits after primary research concludes, Weeks 5 through 7, so the ranges are grounded in what buyers actually said rather than desk research alone, and land in time to inform portfolio prioritization and the financial model that follows.

Alongside sizing, Slalom will benchmark the cost and profit structures typical of the three service pillars to create an initial P&L view. This may include customer acquisition cost and margin ranges for platform and subscription businesses for Digital Products & Platforms, and utilization and margin ranges in advisory and managed-services models for the other two pillars. This is category-level benchmarking, not a claim about any named competitor's specific cost structure, since that level of detail is rarely disclosed publicly. This gives the financial model an external reference point—particularly for Digital Products & Platforms—where the relevant economics may look very different from RS&H's existing AEC cost structure.

Example Outputs: TAM, SAM, and SOM ranges by market and pillar, with documented assumptions, and industry cost and profit benchmarks by pillar.

Expected Outcome

RS&H leadership will receive a transparent, triangulated view of who buys, which problems are urgent enough to fund, how demand and buying-center dynamics differ across Transportation, Aviation, and Federal Defense, and which packaging, delivery, and pricing constructs have the strongest evidence of buyer acceptance across the three service pillars. Findings will be traceable to documented source types, participant profiles, anonymized primary research, and a clearly defined quantitative sample, giving RS&H a defensible evidence base for portfolio prioritization, market sizing, go-to-market design, and financial modeling.

B.5 Business and Financial Models (§3.5)

Slalom's approach to creating an executive-ready financial model

The financial model breaks down into four main components – 1) the demand-side / revenue build, 2) the operation cost build, 3) the capital investment schedule, and 4) P&L / ROI analysis.

1. Demand side and revenue model

Based on the outputs from B.3, B.4, B.5, Slalom will model out the demand volume projections over time based on target markets, customer segments, current / future state operational capabilities, go-to-market channels and motions, and prioritized offerings.

The customer segments and go-to-market motions will inform the prioritized offering revenue models (e.g. SaaS / recurring revenue, transaction based, etc.). The revenue model may include price modeling, product / service bundle scenarios, feature attach rates, etc.

Key deliverables: Demand funnel for each prioritized offering, revenue model for each prioritized offering, demand / growth projection, summarized list of demand side assumptions (e.g. growth rates, attach rates, etc.)

2. Product and service cost structure, and SG&A

The prioritized offerings will inform the product and service cost structure.

For Digital Products and Platforms, Slalom will estimate cost of service (e.g. cloud costs, customer success / support, etc.) based on demand side volume projections and technology infrastructure solution estimates. For SG&A Slalom will model the customer acquisition cost and other sales / marketing costs (assuming this product lines CAC will differ than traditional RS&H business). Slalom will use RS&H's current overhead as reference for G&A. If this product line requires a standalone overhead, Slalom will model out a separate SG&A informed by market comparables and product line overhead requirements.

For Digital Advisory & Consulting, and Digital Managed Services, Slalom will estimate cost of service based on demand side volume projections, technology infrastructure solution estimates, and labor estimates (e.g. fully loaded personnel costs, FF&E per head, etc.). For SG&A Slalom will model the customer acquisition cost and other sales / marketing costs (assuming this service lines CAC will differ than tradition RS&H business). Slalom will use RS&H's current overhead as reference for G&A. If a specific service line (e.g. Digital Managed Services) requires a standalone overhead, Slalom will model out a separate SG&A informed by market comparables and service line overhead requirements.

Key deliverables: Unit cost structure and model for each prioritized offering which covers cost of service and SG&A. If applicable, a separate SG&A model may be developed for offerings that require a standalone cost structure outside of RS&H.
