B.4 Business and Financial Models (§3.5)

Slalom's approach to creating an executive-ready financial model

The financial model breaks down into four main components – 1) the demand side / revenue build, 2) the operation cost build, 3) the capital investment schedule, and 4) P&L / ROI analysis.

1. Demand side and revenue model

Based on the outputs from B.2 and B.3, Slalom will model out the demand volume projections over time based on target markets, customer segments, current / future state operational capabilities, go-to-market channels and motions, and prioritized offerings.

The customer segments and go-to-market motions will inform the prioritized offering revenue models (e.g. SaaS / recurring revenue, transaction based, etc.), and items to deduct (e.g. promotions, partner revenue share, royalties, etc.) from gross service revenue to derive net service revenue. The revenue model may include price modeling, product / service bundle scenarios, feature attach rates, etc.

Example Outputs: Demand funnel for each prioritized offering, revenue model for each prioritized offering, demand / growth projection, summarized list of demand side assumptions (e.g. growth rates, attach rates, etc.)

2. Product and service cost structure, and SG&A

The prioritized offerings will inform the product and service cost structure.

For Digital Products and Platforms, Slalom will estimate cost of service (e.g. cloud costs, customer success / support, etc.) based on demand side volume projections and technology infrastructure solution estimates. For SG&A Slalom will model the customer acquisition cost and other sales / marketing costs (assuming this product lines CAC will differ than traditional RS&H business). Slalom will use RS&H's current overhead as reference for G&A. If this product line requires a standalone overhead, Slalom will model out a separate SG&A informed by market comparables and product line overhead requirements.

For Digital Advisory & Consulting, and Digital Managed Services, Slalom will estimate cost of service based on demand side volume projections, technology infrastructure solution estimates, and labor estimates (e.g. fully loaded personnel costs, FF&E per head, etc.). For SG&A Slalom will model the customer acquisition cost and other sales / marketing costs (assuming this service lines CAC will differ than traditional RS&H business). Slalom will use RS&H's current overhead as reference for G&A. If a specific service line (e.g. Digital Managed Services) requires a standalone overhead, Slalom will model out a separate SG&A informed by market comparables and service line overhead requirements.

Example Outputs: Unit cost structure and model for each prioritized offering which covers cost of service and SG&A. If applicable, a separate SG&A model may be developed for offerings that require a standalone cost structure outside of RS&H.

3. Startup costs and capital expenditures (optional financing / debt sizing)

Based on demand volume projections, product and service asset requirements, and timing of expenses, Slalom will size the capital requirements to support the prioritized offerings. Slalom will also construct a depreciation schedule based on RS&H's internal accounting policies.

Optional - If RS&H decides to seek external financing options, Slalom will size the peak funding requirement based on accumulated free cash flow and adjust the financial model to capture the necessary metrics (e.g. DSCR, liquidity ratios) for reporting.

Example Outputs: CAPEX and depreciation schedule for assets and capital services required to support the prioritized offerings. If applicable, a cash flow sweep module may get added to the model to size debt.

4. Profitability, ROI and scenarios analysis

Based on the demand side and revenue model, cost structure, and CAPEX schedule, Slalom will develop P&Ls for the prioritized products and service lines. From the P&Ls Slalom will summarize the relevant profitability metrics (e.g. GSR, NSR, gross margin, EBBIT margin, etc.), operational metrics (e.g. ARR / MRR, CAC, revenue retention, churn rate, etc.), ROI metrics (e.g. NPV, IRR, ROA, ROIC, etc.), and stated operating horizons (near-term FY27, mid-term FY28, long-term FY30).

Slalom will model out key variables for scenario analysis. Variables may be driven by market dynamics, pricing changes, adoption rates, etc. Slalom will work with project sponsors / stakeholders to identify the variables relevant for RS&H.

Example Outputs: Three statement financial model built from sub P&Ls, consolidated balance sheet, cashflow statement, and CAPEX schedule. Model will also contain a summary page for profitability, operations, and ROI analysis. Model will also include an appendix of assumptions and data sources.

Expected Outcome: We will provide leadership with an evidence-based foundation for decision-making. The completed financial model will support the strategic rationale, expected financial and operational impacts, de-risk investments, implementation requirements, and the conditions required to achieve the projected growth above the $1M NSR today.

B.5 Offering Strategy (§3.3)

B.5.1 Digital Offering Prioritization Framework

To ensure RS&H's offering strategy is focused on the evidence-based best set of digital investments, Slalom will create a digital offering prioritization framework, resulting in a validated portfolio, offerings within each of RS&H's three service pillars, with a clear rationale behind each offering selected as well as those set aside.

1. Future-state vision workshop

Slalom will run a working session with RS&H leadership to establish what DTS needs to look like by 2030 to deliver on Amplify 2030's growth ambition, not just what today's evidence supports. This sets the strategic lens the rest of the framework is measured against. Without it, prioritization defaults to whichever offering has the strongest evidence right now, which can favor familiar capabilities over the ones RS&H needs to build toward its longer-term ambition.

Example Outputs: Documented future-state vision and strategic priorities.

2. Capability maturity and strategic fit assessment

Through a focused round of additional stakeholder interviews, Slalom will assess RS&H's digital capabilities against two references. The first is an industry maturity standard, showing how RS&H's current capabilities compare to what leading firms and digital-native competitors have already built. The second is the future-state vision itself, showing how each capability maps against where RS&H is trying to go, so a capability that looks mature by industry standards but doesn't advance the 2030 ambition gets identified as clearly as one that closes a real gap.

Example Outputs: Capability maturity benchmark and strategic fit map, by capability area.

3. Bringing the evidence together

Scoring draws on the full evidence base at this point: the current-state assessment, the market and buyer research, directional financial modeling, and the future-state vision and strategic fit assessment above. Each plays a distinct role. Current state establishes what RS&H can deliver today. Market research establishes what buyers want and will pay for. Financial modeling establishes what the economics can support. Future-state vision and strategic fit establish where RS&H needs to be, not just where the evidence says it already stands.

4. Digital offering scorecard

To compare candidate offerings on equal footing, Slalom will build a business model canvas for each one, one canvas per offering, showcasing the client problem it solves, the target buyer, the delivery model, standalone-versus-bundled posture, and directional economics from the pillar-level financial work. Slalom will build a lighter canvas for the full long list, enough to score each offering across the four dimensions. For offerings that clear an initial light canvas evaluation, a more comprehensive canvas will be completed.

The comprehensive canvases will then be scored across four dimensions: 1) market attractiveness, 2) RS&H's right to win, 3) commercial potential, and 4) feasibility. Score will be tied to the specific evidence behind it and given a confidence level. Additional weighting may then be applied to calibrate each offering's score against its strategic fit.

Example Outputs: Offering canvases, client-problem-to-offering map, weighted scorecard. Finalized digital offering portfolio with rationale.

B.5.2 Commercialization and GTM Strategy

With a clear view of where the market is going, where RS&H has the right to win, and with which services, the question becomes how. Slalom will design the commercialization and go-to-market playbook RS&H's marketing, sales, and delivery teams will execute against: which accounts to target, which routes and partners to use, and how to talk about each offering, externally and internally. Slalom will establish the strategy. RS&H's own teams will own execution, campaigns, positioning production, and pipeline management beyond the strategy.

B.5.3 Account and Sales Strategy

To ensure RS&H's account and sales teams know how to prioritize their time and effort, who to reach, and when the moment is right, Slalom will identify which of RS&H's priority accounts, top-ten accounts total, are the strongest fit for each of the offerings. Slalom will request existing account and relationship data, current tiering system, current services sold, relationship tenure, known digital needs or triggers, and will match that data against the buying-center patterns from the market research and each offering's target-buyer profile. Where that data isn't readily available, Slalom will run a single working session with RS&H account leadership to identify and rank account fit directly.

Up to 10 accounts, recommended ~3 per market, Slalom will document pipeline-generation guidance by offering type, existing-account opportunity mining, targeted outreach, partner-generated demand, in a short written recommendation RS&H's sales teams can apply directly.

Example Outputs: Account-fit mapping by offering, account-based growth approach, pricing structure based on level of project and type (Fixed rate, Time and materials, Fixed capacity), and pipeline guidance.

B.5.4 Commercial and Delivery Strategy

For each of the offerings, Slalom will document a written commercial recommendation: whether it sells standalone, bundles into design, program management, or construction management work, or is delivered as a managed service. Slalom will document a preliminary pricing recommendation for each offering, structure, relative price position, and margin aspiration, built from the offering's directional economics and the industry benchmark economics by pillar, sufficient to guide commercial conversations and proposal development into the playbook.

The playbook will also include route-to-market recommendations for each offering, direct, partner, white-label, or embedded, and where a partner is the better path, will identify the type of partner needed and the alliance model, co-sell, co-deliver, resell, that fits.

Example Outputs: Bundled-versus-standalone recommendation by offering, preliminary pricing framework by offering, route-to-market recommendation by offering, partner and alliance strategy.

B.5.5 Marketing, Positioning, and Enablement

The playbook's final piece is strengthening the positioning RS&H carries into the market, from external marketing materials through to how sellers and delivery teams talk about each offering internally.

For each of the priority offerings, Slalom will add a high-level positioning brief: the differentiation argument against AEC competitors and digital-native firms, supporting proof points, and a core message, written to guide RS&H's own marketing function in producing external materials, pursuit language, and proposal content. Slalom does not produce designed or published marketing assets.

Slalom will also add internal messaging guidance for executives, sellers, and delivery teams, what the offering is, who buys it, and why RS&H can credibly win it, along with enablement priorities, discovery questions, objection handling, and standalone-versus-bundled guidance, giving RS&H's own sales-enablement materials a starting point.

Example Outputs: High-level positioning brief and internal messaging guidance.

Expected Outcome: RS&H leadership will receive a ranked digital portfolio, chosen against today's evidence and against where RS&H needs to be by 2030, with a clear, defensible rationale behind each offering.
