Section A — Firm Overview & Qualifications

A.1 Firm Profile

Slalom, Inc. is a privately held business and technology consulting firm founded in 1993 and headquartered in Seattle. Our approximately 10,000 employees across 52 offices in 12 countries help Global 1000, mid-market, and public-sector organizations move from strategy through execution, supported by partnerships with more than 700 technology providers.

A.1.1 Conflict of Interest Disclosure (§12.3)

Slalom and RS&H operate in some of the same industries, including transportation, aviation, and adjacent markets, which may create a perceived conflict as Slalom supports RS&H in commercializing its digital offerings. We do not view this overlap as a material conflict; our experience packaging, pricing, and commercializing digital services is directly relevant to the work RS&H is seeking.

To protect RS&H’s interests, Slalom will maintain a dedicated engagement team, apply strict confidentiality and information-handling protocols, and ensure RS&H-specific strategies, deliverables, and proprietary information are not reused for other clients. Executive oversight and conflict reviews will provide additional transparency throughout the engagement.

For RS&H, this means we are well positioned to determine which digital capabilities should become standalone revenue streams, which should strengthen existing services and client relationships, and how each should be commercialized through RS&H’s major-account and pursuit model. Our experience shows that digital twins, AI-enabled tools, connected-asset solutions, platform offerings, and managed services can all be viable growth vehicles when they are tied to real client demand, clear value propositions, and an operating model that can support scale.

A.3 Functional Expertise (§6.2)

Slalom brings repeatable frameworks and integrated expertise that help organizations move from strategy through commercialization by aligning market opportunity assessment, buyer and stakeholder analysis, account segmentation, offering strategy, pricing and packaging, commercialization planning, sales enablement, pursuit support, delivery capabilities, operating-model design, and financial planning. We have applied these frameworks to technology, digital transformation, data, AI, managed-service, and platform offerings that require executive sponsorship, stakeholder alignment, and consultative selling across multiple buying centers. This integrated approach turns collections of digital capabilities into focused, commercially viable growth portfolios supported by demonstrated demand, clear buyers and value propositions, effective sales motions, repeatable delivery models, and economics aligned to RS&H’s growth and margin objectives.

Our portfolio strategy work helps organizations determine which digital capabilities deserve investment, how they should be grouped into market-facing offerings, and whether they should be sold independently, bundled with existing services, or delivered through recurring managed-service or platform models. We evaluate each opportunity against customer demand, differentiation, strategic fit, delivery feasibility, scalability, revenue potential, and margin profile, then translate priorities into defined offerings with target buyers, value propositions, service components, pricing logic, delivery requirements, and investment roadmaps. Slalom has applied this approach in public transportation by combining one-on-one interviews, small-group workshops, an agency-wide survey, analogous-initiative analysis, a capability-based organizational view, and an opportunity-prioritization framework to define transformation pillars, a future-state vision, and a roadmap for execution.

Slalom also brings rigorous market assessment and segmentation capabilities. We combine secondary research with customer and stakeholder interviews, account and pipeline analysis, competitive intelligence, and demand validation to estimate the addressable opportunity and identify the segments most likely to buy. This includes evaluating TAM, SAM, and obtainable market potential; segmenting buyers by needs, behaviors, industry, maturity, and purchasing authority; and testing willingness to pay and preferred commercial models. The result is a fact-based view of where RS&H should compete, which buyers and accounts should be prioritized, and how each offering should be positioned.

Because RS&H operates through major accounts, long sales cycles, multiple buying centers, and relationship-led business development, our approach extends beyond market sizing and offering design to establish a repeatable growth engine aligned to the realities of enterprise buying. Slalom helps organizations integrate digital offerings into strategic account planning, pursuit and capture activities, alliance ecosystems, and relationship-led sales motions. We develop buyer-specific messaging, value propositions, qualification criteria, account plays, pursuit strategies, sales tools, and enablement programs that help account teams recognize opportunities, engage the right stakeholders, and introduce digital capabilities credibly. This allows digital growth to build upon RS&H’s established relationships and business-development model rather than depend on a separate, disconnected sales channel.

We design operating models that specify governance, decision rights, ownership, funding, delivery responsibilities, performance measures, talent requirements, and long-term support boundaries; for one state transportation agency, this included defining ownership across the agency, the statewide IT organization, and third parties while standardizing permitting workflows across six operating districts. Our objective is to provide enterprise consistency while keeping the business units closest to RS&H’s clients actively involved in shaping, selling, and delivering the offerings.

Financial rigor is embedded throughout this work. Slalom develops scenario-based models connecting portfolio decisions to revenue, investment, cost-to-serve, utilization, scalability, margin, and EBITDA/EBBIT outcomes. We test project-based, subscription, managed-service, retainer, transaction-based, and hybrid models to identify the conditions required to reach target profitability and give RS&H a transparent basis for deciding where to invest, what to launch first, and how quickly to scale. Slalom also tests the cost-to-serve implications of technology and delivery choices: for one state transportation agency, a multilingual serverless chatbot and voice solution used elastic, pay-for-use cloud services and was expected to reduce operating costs by up to 99%.
